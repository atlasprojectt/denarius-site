# CONTENT.md — Positioning, Messaging, and Conversion

## Purpose

This file guides product positioning, page narrative, copy, and conversion for the Denarius marketing site.

It defines what the page should communicate, not an exact final script. Fable 5 should use these principles to develop the strongest narrative for the current product, assets, and conversion flow.

All public-facing site copy should be written in natural Brazilian Portuguese unless a task explicitly states otherwise.

## Product in one sentence

Denarius is a B2B AI-spend governance product that helps technology companies understand, attribute, budget, project, and act on their AI costs.

Its core value is control before the invoice, not reporting after the fact.

## Audience

Primary audience:

- CEOs;
- CTOs;
- finance leaders;
- operations or FinOps leaders;
- technology companies with roughly 20–200 employees.

This audience is busy, skeptical, and sensitive to financial risk. It does not need more AI enthusiasm. It needs a credible answer to an operational problem.

## Audience tension

The page should reflect the real tension:

- AI adoption spreads faster than cost ownership;
- spend is distributed across providers, teams, API projects, and subscriptions;
- invoices arrive after the opportunity to intervene;
- token counts do not answer budget questions;
- leaders need to know where the month is heading, not only what has already happened;
- companies want governance without installing a gateway or creating employee-surveillance dynamics.

Do not dramatize the problem beyond what the product can support.

## Desired belief

By the time the user reaches the main CTA, they should believe:

- Denarius gives the company a clear operating view of AI spend;
- it can fit into the existing workflow without replacing providers;
- it turns fragmented usage into budget context;
- it surfaces risk early enough to support a decision;
- its numbers and data boundaries are handled carefully;
- adopting it feels proportionate to the problem.

## Communication pillars

The page should build its argument around these pillars. Their order and presentation may change.

### 1. Visibility

Make distributed spend legible.

Possible proof areas:

- company-wide spend;
- spend by provider or source;
- spend by team;
- unattributed expenses;
- manual subscriptions and seats.

### 2. Governance

Connect spend to an explicit limit.

Possible proof areas:

- organization and team budgets;
- pacing against the current period;
- projected margin;
- clear status or verdict;
- budget ownership.

### 3. Foresight

Help the user see where the month is heading.

Possible proof areas:

- closing projection;
- projected budget breach;
- trend and pace;
- early warnings;
- data freshness and estimation honesty.

### 4. Action

Move from observation to investigation.

Possible proof areas:

- prioritized issues;
- contextual next actions;
- top cost drivers;
- team investigation;
- scenario simulation.

### 5. Trust

Reduce the perceived risk of connecting company data.

Possible proof areas:

- read-only connections;
- no prompt or response access;
- transparent freshness;
- explicit unattributed or uncosted values;
- careful handling of company data;
- honest product boundaries.

Features should serve these pillars. Do not present a flat inventory of everything the product can do.

## Narrative guidance

The page should have a clear progression, but the final section structure is open.

A strong narrative will usually move through some version of:

1. establish the control problem;
2. state the product outcome;
3. demonstrate how Denarius creates clarity;
4. show how it fits into the existing workflow;
5. prove governance, projection, and action;
6. address trust and data boundaries;
7. close with a confident next step.

The hero should not explain the entire product. It should earn the user's attention and make the next section feel necessary.

The middle of the page should provide evidence, not repeat the hero in different words.

The closing should reinforce the decision-relevant outcome rather than summarizing every feature.

## Message direction

Good message territory includes:

- knowing how the month is likely to close;
- controlling spend before the invoice;
- turning scattered AI costs into an operating view;
- giving each team's spend budget context;
- finding the few issues that require action;
- creating governance without a gateway;
- making AI cost a manageable variable rather than an opaque one.

These are territories for exploration, not mandatory headlines.

Fable 5 should develop the final hierarchy and phrasing based on the page structure.

## Tone

The voice should be:

- concise;
- calm;
- confident;
- specific;
- financially literate;
- operational;
- technically honest;
- free of hype.

Use short, complete statements. Prefer concrete nouns and verbs to adjectives.

The copy may be elegant, but it should not become vague.

## CTA strategy

The site should have one consistent primary conversion goal.

Choose the CTA according to the real destination:

- direct product entry;
- access request;
- contact or sales flow;
- product walkthrough.

The label should communicate what happens next. A convincing CTA is not necessarily aggressive; it reduces uncertainty.

Possible CTA directions in Portuguese may include:

- `Começar`;
- `Começar a controlar`;
- `Solicitar acesso`;
- `Ver o produto`;
- `Entender como funciona`.

These are examples, not fixed requirements.

A secondary CTA should reveal product value or workflow, not send the user to generic content.

Use the same primary action in the hero and closing unless the user journey genuinely changes.

Support CTA moments with risk-reducing context when useful, such as the read-only model or the absence of prompt access.

## Proof strategy

Prefer product evidence over decorative claims.

Useful proof can include:

- a real product interface;
- focused UI crops;
- a clear workflow;
- an annotated outcome;
- specific product behavior;
- concise explanations of data handling;
- transparent operational boundaries.

If customer proof does not exist, do not imitate a mature social-proof section. Product clarity and trust can carry the page.

## Language principles

Prefer language that answers:

- What changes for the user?
- What can they know sooner?
- What becomes easier to control?
- What action can they take?
- Why should they trust the process?

Avoid overusing the product name in every sentence.

Avoid explaining features with internal implementation terminology unless it strengthens trust.

Use English product terms only when the Portuguese alternative would be less natural for the target audience.

## Precise copy prohibitions

Do not use:

- "Revolucione sua empresa";
- "Transforme o futuro";
- "Desbloqueie todo o potencial";
- "A solução definitiva";
- "Tudo o que você precisa";
- "Insights poderosos" without naming the insight;
- "Controle total";
- "Economize instantaneamente";
- "Nunca mais ultrapasse o orçamento";
- "Elimine desperdícios" as an absolute promise;
- "Tempo real" for daily data;
- "IA que decide por você";
- "Monitoramento de funcionários";
- "Sem risco";
- "100% seguro";
- unsupported savings percentages;
- unsupported ROI claims;
- false urgency;
- countdown timers;
- exclamation-heavy copy;
- jargon used only to sound technical.

Do not frame read-only governance as enforcement.

Do not imply that Denarius has access to prompts or content.

Do not use fear as the primary conversion mechanism.

## Claim boundaries

Safe areas to communicate, when supported by the current product:

- spend visibility;
- attribution;
- budgets;
- projections;
- alerts;
- contextual investigation;
- read-only provider connections;
- metadata-only positioning;
- no prompt or response access.

Claims about encryption, tenant isolation, compliance, connectors, notification channels, or exact data freshness must match the actual implementation and current product documentation.

## Conversion review

Before approving copy, verify:

- the opening names the category or problem clearly;
- the value proposition is understandable without product jargon;
- each section advances the argument;
- claims are concrete enough to be credible;
- feature descriptions connect to executive outcomes;
- the workflow feels adoptable;
- trust concerns are answered before the final CTA;
- CTA labels match their destinations;
- no section relies on invented proof;
- the Portuguese sounds written for Brazil, not translated from English.
